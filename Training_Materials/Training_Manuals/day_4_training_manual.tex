\chapter{Morning Session: Multi-Criteria Policy Analysis (MCPA)}

\section{The Core Dilemma: Policy Prescriptions vs. Policy Options}
In executive ministries, policy briefs often push a single preferred ministerial agenda. In contrast, the Parliamentary Research Service must serve the entire National Assembly: the Speaker, committee chairs, backbenchers, ruling party members, and the opposition. 

If a parliamentary researcher submits a brief asserting:
\begin{center}
    \textit{"The Government must immediately reintroduce price controls and ban all grain exports,"}
\end{center}
the researcher has abandoned professional detachment and entered the realm of partisan policy advocacy. If the researcher instead presents:
\begin{center}
    \textit{"A strategic multi-criteria evaluation of three alternative policy options to stabilize national grain availability during regional supply deficits,"}
\end{center}
systematically evaluating the fiscal expenditure, administrative friction, market distortions, and statutory mandates of each alternative, the researcher provides true democratic empowerment.

\section{The 4-Pillar Multi-Criteria Policy Analysis (MCPA) Framework}
To ensure that policy alternatives are evaluated systematically and without bias, \textbf{AB Consult} applies a four-pillar assessment standard tailored for the Parliament of Tanzania:

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/fig_day4_mcpa_pillars.png}
  \caption{The 4-Pillar MCPA Evaluation Architecture for Legislative Oversight.}
  \label{fig:mcpa_pillars}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Detailed Breakdown of the 4 Evaluation Criteria}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Fiscal Viability:} What does this option cost the Consolidated Fund? Is it a one-time capital appropriation or an open-ended recurrent subsidy? Does it create sovereign debt liabilities or divert resources from priority sectors?
    \item \textbf{Administrative Implementability:} Does the public bureaucracy possess the operational capacity to enforce the policy? For instance, does a centralized electronic warehouse receipt system function in rural wards with intermittent cellular coverage?
    \item \textbf{Socio-Economic Impact:} Who benefits and who bears the economic burden? A policy that maximizes central government tax revenue may inadvertently suppress rural household farm-gate income or discourage private agro-processing investments.
    \item \textbf{Statutory Alignment:} Does the proposed option require primary legislation (an Act of Parliament) or subsidiary legislation (a Gazette Notice / Ministerial Regulation)? Does it conform to East African Community (EAC) Common Market Protocols regarding free movement of commodities?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Formulating Defensible, Non-Partisan Recommendations}
When formulating recommendations for Members of Parliament, adhere to the **3-Tier Recommendation Architecture**:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Primary Action (The Immediate Strategic Path):} Identify the option with the highest balance of feasibility and impact, framed as a balanced recommendation for committee consideration.
    \item \textbf{Contingency / Hybrid Mechanism:} Propose how elements of another option can mitigate the primary option's weaknesses (e.g., combining private warehouse credits with targeted smallholder storage subsidies).
    \item \textbf{Oversight \& Monitoring Milestone:} Define specific legislative questions, statutory audits, or ministerial reporting deadlines that the committee should establish to monitor execution.
\end{enumerate}
