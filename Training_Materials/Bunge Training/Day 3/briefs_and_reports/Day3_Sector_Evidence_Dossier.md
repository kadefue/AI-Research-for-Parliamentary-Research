## 1. Executive Callout — The Decision Anchor

**The delivery shortfall is not measurable from the evidence supplied: it is not zero, but unknown.** The 2025 Statistical Abstract counts 9,955,355 agricultural households nationally, yet neither it nor the 2024/25 Budget Execution Report states how many farmers were registered for fertilizer support, redeemed e-vouchers, received inputs on time, or remained unserved. Nor do the reports disclose physical fertilizer reserve stocks, reserve-release rules, or dealer-claim ageing. This is an oversight and seasonal-buffer risk: Parliament cannot test whether procurement, distribution, and reimbursement reached eligible farmers before planting. The reported USD 5,971.5 million in gross official foreign-exchange reserves (4.8 months of imports in June 2025) is a macroeconomic indicator—not evidence of fertilizer stocks or a dedicated input reserve. Require a reconciled, location- and date-specific delivery ledger before accepting any delivery-rate claim.

## 2. Strategic and Statutory Framework

The **Fertilizers Act, 2009 (Act No. 9 of 2009), section 4**, assigns the Tanzania Fertilizer Regulatory Authority regulatory functions that include quality control; registration and licensing of fertilizer dealers and premises; import/export permits; maintenance and publication of registers; and control of fertilizer importation, production, transportation, dealing, storage, and disposal. The Act also provides for fertilizer information, stakeholder training, and guidance on sound fertilizer management. These duties make dealer integrity, traceability, storage, and transport central oversight matters—not merely procurement logistics. [Fertilizers Act, s. 4](https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/2009/9/eng@2023-12-31).

FYDP III (2021/22–2025/26) sets the wider direction of **“Realising Competitiveness and Industrialisation for Human Development”** and links stronger productive capacity and agricultural performance to inclusive economic development. For committee purposes, the cited **Key Result Area 2 (KRA 2)** should therefore be tested through verifiable farmer-level access and timely input use, not only budget release or national supply totals. The supplied PDFs do not reproduce a KRA 2 results matrix or fertilizer-subsidy targets; the Ministry should table the governing KRA indicator, baseline, annual target, and responsible vote before performance is certified. [FYDP III, Ministry of Finance and Planning](https://repository.mof.go.tz/items/43b073c7-6944-4a17-ada6-0dc72e71f9cf).

**Interpretation:** “Not reported” is a finding about the evidence, not an estimate of zero. The NBS Statistical Abstract reports 9,824,293 agricultural households on Mainland, 131,063 in Zanzibar, and 9,955,355 nationally in 2024/25. These are agricultural-activity household counts—not the fertilizer programme’s eligible or registered beneficiary list—and must not be substituted as its denominator. A defensible shortfall is **registered eligible beneficiaries minus unique beneficiaries served**; delivery rate is **unique beneficiaries served ÷ registered eligible beneficiaries × 100**. The two source reports provide neither input.

Other indicators help assess the stakes but cannot fill that gap. NBS reports national maize production rising from about 7.44 million tonnes in 2023/24 to 10.02 million tonnes in 2024/25, while reported maize yields fell from 1.9 to 1.5 tonnes/ha in the long rains and from 1.6 to 1.2 tonnes/ha in the short rains. Changing planted area, weather, and other factors prevent attributing either movement to fertilizer delivery. (NBS, *Statistical Abstract 2025*, Tables 6.10–6.11, pp. 52–53; PDF pp. 76–77.)

## 4. Bottleneck Triangulation

The three bottlenecks identified for this review are **credible failure pathways, not confirmed findings in the supplied PDFs**. Neither source reports voucher transaction failures, claim-level dealer reimbursement delays, or dispatch-to-farm delivery times.

- **E-voucher telecommunications:** Weak or intermittent signal, failed USSD/SMS authentication, or platform downtime can block redemption even where fertilizer is available. The Statistical Abstract reports 106.9 million mobile/fixed subscriptions nationally and mobile-data subscriptions, but these aggregates do not establish reliable coverage, affordability, or transaction success in farming communities (Tables 10.11–10.13, pp. 94–96). Test failed transactions by ward, operator, date, and failure code against eligible farmers and completed redemptions.
- **Agro-dealer reimbursement:** Delayed validation or settlement can tie up dealer working capital, discourage further participation, and leave vouchers nominally redeemable but inputs unavailable. The Budget Execution Report records TZS 113.58 billion paid against **all** verified “Supplies (Goods & Services)” arrears in 2024/25; it does not identify fertilizer dealers or current voucher claims. Do not treat this aggregate as proof of a fertilizer backlog. Reconcile each claim’s submission, approval, payment, rejection reason, and age.
- **Transport and last-mile delivery:** Late release from port, central stores, or regional depots can miss planting windows and strand stock away from beneficiaries. The reports contain no fertilizer consignments, delivery milestones, stock-out days, or seasonal lead-time data. Match purchase orders and dispatch notes to warehouse receipts and farmer handover dates; disaggregate by route, district, and planting calendar.

Triangulation requires a shared transaction key linking beneficiary register, voucher redemption, dealer invoice/claim, payment, and physical dispatch/receipt. Without that join, a large redemption count can coexist with unpaid dealers or late, incomplete deliveries.

## 5. Strategic Policy Implications for Committee Interrogation

**Fiscal exposure.** Government-wide 2024/25 expenditure was TZS 49,362.28 billion against TZS 50,291.41 billion (98%); TZS 1,128.85 billion was paid against verified arrears, including TZS 113.58 billion for supplies and services. These totals do not establish fertilizer-program spending or dealer liabilities. Request the programme’s approved allocation, releases, commitments, unpaid claims, cancellations, unit subsidy, and outstanding stock valuation, reconciled to Treasury and dealer records. The June 2025 foreign-exchange reserve position is a macro buffer, not proof that fertilizer procurement is funded or physically reserved. (Ministry of Finance, *Budget Execution Report 2024/25*, pp. 7, 16.)

**Production-loss risk.** A delivery failure during a planting window can reduce input use and yield, but the available crop statistics do not identify fertilizer access or causality. Require the Ministry to quantify late and unserved beneficiaries by crop and season, then link that record to sampled input-use and yield data. Do not book a production-loss figure without a transparent counterfactual and assumptions.

**Recommended summons and returns.** Summon the **Permanent Secretary, Ministry of Agriculture**, to table the complete beneficiary-to-delivery ledger and KRA 2 scorecard; the **Director General, Tanzania Fertilizer Regulatory Authority**, to produce dealer/licence registers, quality inspections, storage and transport oversight, and reserve-stock records; and the **Treasury/Accountant General** with the responsible programme accounting officer to reconcile appropriation, payment, and arrears. Require the e-voucher platform operator and relevant telecom providers to provide anonymized transaction uptime/failure data, and regional/council agriculture officers and contracted transporters to certify dated dispatch, receipt, stock-out, and farmer handover records. Set a single reporting cut-off and require exceptions to be independently reconciled before the next seasonal allocation.
